# Mandi2Door business plan: Balaji Symphony (about 2,500 families)

## 1. Why this works
- **One society, many flats**: one delivery person covers 60–100 flats in 2 hours. Quick-commerce apps spend far more per delivery.
- **Orders come in the day before**, so you buy exactly what's sold. Wastage drops from the usual 10–15% for vendors to about 3%.
- **APMC prices are 30–45% below quick-commerce prices** for veg and fruit. You can share about half that gap with residents and keep the rest.

## 2. Unit economics (sample prices in the app)
| | Value |
|---|---|
| Average basket | ₹200–250 |
| Gross margin on veg & fruit | 25–30% of selling price |
| Margin on milk | ₹1.5–3 per pouch (low; milk is there to bring people back every day) |
| Margin on eggs | 15–20% |
| **Profit per order after wastage** | **about ₹40–50** |
| Daily fixed cost (1 person + tempo + bags) | about ₹1,400 |
| **Break-even** | **about 30–35 orders/day** (just over 1% of the society) |

## 3. Growth targets
| Month | Active families | Orders/day | Milk subs | Staff | Net profit/month (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 35–50 | 40 | 1 + you | ₹0–15k (break-even) |
| 3 | 300 | 120–150 | 150 | 2 | ₹1–1.4 lakh |
| 6 | 600+ | 250–300 | 300 | 3–4 | ₹2.5–3.5 lakh |

**Capacity check:** one person can handle buying, packing and delivering about **60–80 orders** a day. Plan to hire a second person at around 70 orders/day, and help with packing yourself in the first month.

## 4. Sourcing
- **Vegetables & fruit**: APMC (Vashi, or Panvel APMC if it's closer). Build relationships with 2–3 commission agents (*adatyas*). Ask for their best-quality lots and pay cash the same day for better rates. Buying 50–150 kg a day gets you near-wholesale prices.
- **Milk**: a local dairy distributor (Gokul, Amul, Chitale, Warana etc.) delivers at about 5 AM at distributor rates. Never run out of milk: if it's late, residents will leave.
- **Eggs**: a wholesale egg trader buying by the tray (Namakkal/local poultry). Rates change daily; check NECC rates.
- **Hygiene**: wash and sort leafy greens, and use crates rather than sacks. Quality is what keeps people ordering; low price only gets the first order.

## 5. Hiring the delivery person
- **Profile**: local, honest, has a smartphone with WhatsApp and UPI, two-wheeler or cycle with crates, available 4–9 AM. Ideally someone known to society security.
- **Pay**: ₹15,000–18,000/month for the morning shift, plus ₹2/order incentive after 60 orders/day.
- **Rules**: police verification, society gate pass, Mandi2Door T-shirt and ID card. Collect UPI only to the business QR, never cash to a personal account.
- **Daily check**: total UPI received = total marked "Paid" in the app.

## 6. Launch plan (first 30 days)
1. **Week 0**: Get permission from the society committee for gate entry and a small sorting spot (parking corner or podium). Offer the committee a discount for society events.
2. **Week 0**: FSSAI **Basic Registration** (₹100/year; needed for food businesses with turnover up to ₹12 lakh; move to a State License above that). Open a current account with a UPI QR in the business name.
3. **Week 1**: Soft launch in 2–3 buildings through their WhatsApp groups. Offer ₹50 off the first order and free delivery for the first week.
4. **Week 2**: Put a stall in the lobby on Sunday morning showing the price comparison board ("Blinkit ₹52 vs Mandi2Door ₹36"). Sign residents up for milk subscriptions on the spot.
5. **Week 3–4**: Referrals: ₹30 credit for both sides. Recruit "building captains" (one active resident per building) for a free item each week.

## 7. Risks and how to handle them
| Risk | Plan |
|---|---|
| Quality complaint | Replace free the next morning, no questions asked. Track complaints by item |
| APMC price spike (tomato/onion) | Update price the same day; the app always shows it's still cheaper than Blinkit |
| Delivery person absent | Train a backup person; you cover the route |
| Unpaid orders | Block new orders for flats with dues over ₹500 (check the Delivery tab) |
| Cash flow | APMC is cash/same-day. Keep ₹15–20k float; milk subscriptions bring in a lump sum each month |

## 8. Tax notes (confirm with a CA)
- Fresh vegetables, fruit, eggs and fresh (unpacked) milk are **GST-exempt**. Branded curd and paneer carry 5% GST.
- If you sell only exempt goods, GST registration is not required. Get advice once paneer/curd sales or turnover grow.

## 9. Later
Add staples (atta, rice, dal, oil) with the same comparison model, a weekend "exotic veg" basket, and repeat the model in a second society nearby once one society is profitable.
